Chaining a trusted redirect across an OAuth allowlist

Auth callback validation · cross-domain trust boundaries

I investigated a redirect-validation system that rejected non-allowlisted callback destinations but appeared to accept a trusted intermediary that could forward navigation onward. My technical argument focused on the authentication service trusting the first hop.

RESEARCH TYPEReal investigation · sanitized
REVIEW OUTCOMEOut of scope
EVIDENCE LEVELEvidence: technical rationale and triage decision preserved
01 / Research question

The hypothesis

A callback allowlist may validate the supplied host while ignoring where a trusted first hop ultimately sends the browser.

Sequence under investigation

  1. Auth service checks callback allowlist
  2. First destination is trusted
  3. Trusted page forwards to an external destination
  4. Browser ends beyond the intended trust boundary

03 / Security reasoning

Which invariant matters?

A callback validation policy must account for actual navigation semantics, rather than assuming an allowlisted intermediary cannot change the final destination.

What the source record supports

The archive shows my argument about the auth service whitelist and an error identifier on direct disallowed callbacks. I explained that the intermediary was a chain component, not the core trust decision.

04 / Reviewer feedback

Where the proof ended

Triage viewed the redirect intermediary as explicitly out of scope and maintained the rejection even after my clarification. The export does not establish token leakage or unauthorized account access.

RECORDED RESULT

Closed out of scope after an explicit re-evaluation discussion.

05 / Engineering takeaway

What changed in my methodology

A technically interesting trusted-hop chain is not enough. State the exact allowlist control, the final destination, and whether each intermediate component is authorized for testing.
