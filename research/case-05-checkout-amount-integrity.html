Client-controlled payment amount and server-side pricing

Payment session creation · business logic integrity

This investigation asked whether the amount supplied during payment-session creation could override the authoritative price for a paid product. The submitted category was business-logic error and the report was classified under paywall bypass by triage.

RESEARCH TYPEReal investigation · sanitized
REVIEW OUTCOMEOut of scope

01 / Research question

The hypothesis

If the server accepts a client-provided amount when creating a payment session, checkout integrity could depend on untrusted data rather than a server-side price catalogue.

Sequence under investigation

  1. Choose an advertised paid product
  2. Intercept payment-session creation
  3. Alter client-controlled amount
  4. Compare provider authorization and server entitlement state

03 / Security reasoning

Which invariant matters?

Derive billable totals and the entitlement granted from trusted server-side order data. Confirm payment amount and currency on provider webhook before activating access.

What the source record supports

The report title described arbitrary amount manipulation in a payment-session flow. The export preserves the endpoint type and closure reason but no raw request, response or finished settlement receipt.

04 / Reviewer feedback

Where the proof ended

Triage rejected the submission under an explicit paywall-bypass exclusion. Without a verified settled charge and entitlement, the impact remains a hypothesis, not a proven purchase bypass.

RECORDED RESULT

Closed out of scope. No confirmed underpriced transaction in the available record.

05 / Engineering takeaway

What changed in my methodology

Payment testing must prove a full lifecycle: request input → provider session → settled payment → entitlement state, with appropriate authorization and financial safeguards.
