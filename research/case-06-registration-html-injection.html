Tracking unsanitized HTML from registration to outbound content

Registration forms · stored content · notification rendering

The submission explored HTML input accepted by a registration workflow and a possible path into later email content. It was submitted as a blind injection issue, with concerns about email-based phishing presentation.

RESEARCH TYPEReal investigation · sanitized
REVIEW OUTCOMEOut of scope
EVIDENCE LEVELEvidence: finding title and program policy response only
01 / Research question

The hypothesis

Untrusted user input entered during registration may be re-used in a downstream HTML rendering context without context-appropriate encoding.

Sequence under investigation

  1. Supply markup in user-controlled registration field
  2. Record whether the application stores it
  3. Follow subsequent notification/template generation
  4. Check the rendered output in a controlled mailbox

03 / Security reasoning

Which invariant matters?

Encode user-provided data according to the final output context, including HTML email templates, and avoid interpreting user-controlled markup as trusted template content.

What the source record supports

The archive confirms the research topic, classification and triage response but does not include raw original payloads or a delivered email artifact.

04 / Reviewer feedback

Where the proof ended

The testing program explicitly listed HTML injection in the affected registration/webform area as out of scope; triage said they were already working on a solution.

RECORDED RESULT

Closed out of scope. No claim of successful phishing email delivery.

05 / Engineering takeaway

What changed in my methodology

For stored injection, prove the complete data flow from input to sink. The relevant evidence is output-context rendering, not just acceptance of an input string.
